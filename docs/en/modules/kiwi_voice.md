# kiwi_voice — source

[HEID//R](../../../README.md) · [Documentation](../README.md) · [Modules](../README.md#modules) · [Русский](../../ru/modules/kiwi_voice.md)

## What it does

Borrows somebody else's shortwave receiver. It picks a frequency in a broadcast
band, finds public KiwiSDR receivers that can hear it and have a free slot,
listens on several of them in turn, plays what arrives and transcribes it.

`sw_voice` needs a dongle and hears your own aerial. This hears an aerial in
Tarlee, or Rotorua, or Borås, and it needs nothing plugged in at all.

## Where the data comes from

The receivers are real KiwiSDR radios that their owners have put on the public
internet. There are roughly nine hundred of them at any time.

The list comes from `rx.linkfanel.net/kiwisdr_com.js`, which is regenerated
about once an hour from the official directory. The official page cannot be
read by a program — it is behind a browser challenge — while the mirror is one
plain array carrying each receiver's address, the range of frequencies it
covers, and how many of its slots are taken.

The audio comes from the receiver itself, through `kiwirecorder.py`.

## How it is processed

### Choosing a receiver

The key's seed picks one metre band and then one frequency inside it, on the
five kilohertz grid the broadcasters use. Only then is the list read, and a
receiver is kept if it is up, if its published range covers that frequency, and
if it has a free slot.

A receiver with all its slots taken is passed over rather than knocked at. Eight
is the whole allowance of a typical KiwiSDR and the people already on it were
there first. For the same reason no two stops land on the same radio: two
connections to one receiver would spend two of its slots hearing the same
frequency twice.

### Listening

`kiwirecorder.py` connects, tunes, demodulates and writes sixteen kilohertz mono
audio for as long as it is told to. Each block goes three ways on the same turn
of the loop — the speaker, the spectrum for the waterfall, and the buffer for
the recogniser — so the picture, the sound and the text are the same moment of
radio.

A receiver that refuses, or that somebody claimed between reading the list and
connecting, simply gives nothing and the next one is tried. That is why more
receivers are chosen than are needed.

### Transcription and anchors

As for the other voice sources: finished phrases become the material, and if the
question produced anchors the phrases carrying those words are kept — unless
none do, in which case everything is.

## Settings

| Key | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `band` | the five metre bands | One is drawn per rite |
| `stops` | 3 | How many receivers to hear from |
| `dwell_s` | 6 | Seconds on each |
| `pool` | 12 | How many receivers to line up, so a busy one costs nothing |
| `mode` | `am` | Demodulation. Shortwave broadcasting is AM |
| `rate` | 16000 | Sample rate |
| `bins` | 64 | Bars in the spectrum |
| `binary` | — | Path to `kiwirecorder.py`; empty finds it on the path |

## Dependencies

A working network, a configured speech provider, and `kiwirecorder.py` from
[kiwiclient](https://github.com/jks-prv/kiwiclient) on the path. Missing any of
the three keeps the module out of the choice. No radio hardware is needed.

Capabilities: `net`, [`stt`](../stt.md).

## Sources

The receivers belong to the amateurs who run them, and this module is a guest on
them. It takes one slot at a time, leaves a full receiver alone, and does not
return to the same radio twice in one rite. Audio-only clients are what the
KiwiSDR firmware expects: it keeps the first two channels for people using a
browser and gives the rest to programs. Some owners set session and daily
limits; those are theirs to set and this module does not work around them.

`kiwirecorder.py` is [kiwiclient](https://github.com/jks-prv/kiwiclient) by John
Seamons. **It carries no licence file**, so none of it is copied here — it is
run as a separate program, the way `rtl_433` and `noaa-apt` are, and its terms
reach nothing in this repository.

The receiver list is generated by [dyatlov](https://github.com/priyom/dyatlov),
GPL-3.0, and published at `rx.linkfanel.net`. Only its output is read.

Choosing one metre band per rite, and letting the dwell time decide what is
caught, is the same design as
[gqrx-ghostbox](https://github.com/DougHaber/gqrx-ghostbox) by Douglas Haber,
BSD-3-Clause.
